Integration & implementation 11 partners

Eleven partner systems. One working platform.

Three payment providers, WeChat sign-in and Order Centre, WeCom single sign-on for staff, an ERP and a store platform for fulfilment, and Alibaba Cloud for SMS, media and video. For each one the routine was the same: read the API document and the contract, prove it in a sandbox, implement the signing scheme when no SDK existed, then handle the parts that break in production — late callbacks, retries, idempotency, refunds — and write down how it works for whoever comes next.

Turning a conversation into a scope.

The owner described goals, not features. I mapped how members, dealers, orders and money actually moved, then shaped that into 17 product areas with a phased plan — storefront first, loyalty and dealer tools next, content and training after.

Go-live & support 0 downtime

A readiness checklist before launch; health checks, runbooks and audit logs after. When the production database had to move, I planned it for zero downtime because dealers place orders during business hours. First line of support for the client's staff ever since.

Judgement

Decisions that mattered.

Four calls that shaped the product more than any feature did — what the problem was, what I decided, and what happened.

Self-service over dependency

A page builder, so marketing never waits for a developer.

Problem
Every campaign meant a new homepage layout, and every layout meant a request to whoever could code — days of delay for a banner.
Decision
Build a drag-and-drop page builder into the console instead of coding pages on request. It cost more upfront and was the biggest single piece of the project.
Result
The marketing team publishes campaigns and landing pages themselves, with no developer in the loop.
Configuration over code

Commission rules the business can change itself.

Problem
The incentive scheme — what commissions are based on, when they pay out, who funds level-difference awards — was still being negotiated while the platform was being built.
Decision
Treat the rules as settings, not code. Every variable the owner might change became a switch in the console, with reversal built in for mistakes.
Result
Changing the incentive scheme is a setting in the console, not a software release — and a mistake can be reversed.
Risk over convenience

A database migration with no downtime — because dealers work in the daytime.

Problem
Production data lived in files and had to move to a proper database. The easy way was a maintenance window; dealers place orders throughout the day and the owner did not want to tell them to stop.
Decision
Run both stores side by side and switch over per data type behind a setting that could be reversed instantly — slower to build, safe to fail.
Result
Nobody noticed. Zero downtime, no records lost, and a rollback switch ready if anything had gone wrong.
Automate the exception

Refund late payments automatically instead of reconciling them by hand.

Problem
Occasionally a customer cancelled and the payment arrived seconds later anyway. Each case became a support ticket, a manual refund and a note for the accountant.
Decision
Detect any payment that lands against a non-pending order and refund it through the original channel, with a ledger entry — no human in the loop unless it fails.
Result
No more manual refunds for late payments, and the books reconcile without anyone touching them.

Approach

How I work with a business.

Habits from a year of being the only person between an owner's ambitions and a working product.

  1. 01

    Listen for the problem, not the feature

    "We need a coupon system" usually means "our dealers are losing customers to price". Solve the second thing.

  2. 02

    Write it down before building it

    Scope, decisions and trade-offs go into documents people can disagree with. Sixteen of them, plus a guide, on this project alone.

  3. 03

    Ship small, ship often

    Fifty releases beat one big launch. Each one is a chance for the business to say "not quite" while it is still cheap.

  4. 04

    Make the business self-sufficient

    Page builder, configurable rules, an operations guide. Success is when the client stops needing to call me.

  5. 05

    Respect the money and the data

    Payments reconcile, refunds are traceable, migrations don't lose records, and sensitive information is masked by default.

  6. 06

    Own the outcome

    Not the ticket, the outcome. If the launch slips because a form wasn't filed, filing the form is my job too.
